# Pricing strategy and competitive positioning

Recorded so the pricing thinking isn't re-done from scratch. These are
grounded starting points to **test with real firms**, not gospel — pricing
is validated in sales conversations, not spreadsheets.

Last updated: 6 July 2026. Competitor figures are indicative (from public
pricing pages / review sites) and move over time.

---

## 1. The key market insight

There are two different categories, and we are in the *lucrative* one:

- **Compliance / filing tools** (UK incumbents: TaxCalc, BTCSoftware,
  Capium, IRIS). These file returns. They are cheap and modular because
  they are a cost centre for the firm:
  - TaxCalc — modular, ~£156/yr accounts production; ~£200 for 12 returns.
  - BTCSoftware — from <£38/mo; ~£65/mo unlimited SA + CT practice.
  - Capium — £135/mo (≤100 clients) → £405/mo (≤500) → custom.
  - IRIS — enterprise, contact-sales.
- **Tax-planning / advisory tools** (mostly US: Corvee, TaxPlanIQ). These
  help the firm *earn advisory fees*, so they command far more:
  - Corvee — ~$150–400/user/mo, up to $500–1,500/mo for firms (credit-based
    per plan).
  - TaxPlanIQ — firms using it **charge their clients $4,800–$10,000 per
    tax plan**; the software pays for itself many times over.
- **UK advisory/reporting** (Fathom ~$50/mo, Futrli, Syft) — forecasting
  and dashboards, *not* tax planning.

**The gap we sit in:** a UK-specific, legislation-cited, defensible tax
*planning* tool for accountants. The UK incumbents do compliance; the UK
advisory tools do reporting; the deep planning tools are US-tax-only. That
is a genuinely underserved position — and it's an advisory (revenue) tool,
so it should be priced as one, not as a cheap compliance add-on.

## 2. Recommended pricing (per firm, monthly, ex VAT)

Tiered by number of users, with an annual discount and a founding-cohort
offer to seed testimonials.

| Tier | For | Users | Monthly | Annual (2 months free) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | Sole practitioner / very small practice | 1 | £59 | £590/yr |
| **Practice** *(sweet spot)* | Small–mid firm | up to 5 | £149 | £1,490/yr |
| **Firm** | Larger practice | unlimited / custom | £349+ | custom |

- **Founding cohort:** first ~10 firms at **50% off for 12 months** in
  exchange for a named case study / testimonial and feedback. This buys
  social proof, which for a new vendor is worth more than the revenue.
- **Free pilot:** offer a 30-day pilot on the demo (their anonymised data or
  the built-in personas) — near-zero cost to you, removes the risk barrier.
- Keep it **per firm/user, not per plan.** Corvee's per-plan credit model is
  widely disliked; flat, predictable pricing is a differentiator.

## 3. The ROI story (how to justify the price)

Anchor every conversation to the *revenue it unlocks*, not the cost:

- At **£149/mo (£1,788/yr)**, the tool pays for itself if the firm lands a
  single paid planning engagement. Even a modest UK planning fee is
  £500–£2,000 per client (the US benchmark is far higher).
- Illustrative: a firm converts **10 compliance clients** to a paid
  £750 "profit-extraction / tax health check" → **£7,500/yr new advisory
  revenue** from £1,788 of software. That is the whole pitch.
- It also *saves* risk: every figure is cited to legislation and
  reproducible, which is a professional-indemnity and defensibility story
  the flashy tools underplay.

## 4. Positioning one-liner

"Productise tax planning: turn your compliance clients into advisory fees,
with advice you can defend line-by-line to HMRC." Sell the revenue and the
defensibility — never compete on being a cheap filing add-on.

## 5. Open questions to test in the field

- Is per-user or a flat per-firm price easier for UK firms to say yes to?
- Right entry price — is £59 too low (signals "cheap tool") or right for
  land-and-expand? Test £59 vs £79.
- Does a per-client tier (like Capium) resonate more than per-user?
- Bundle vs à la carte (e.g. IHT/property modules as add-ons)?

Update this doc as real quotes and objections come back from firms.
